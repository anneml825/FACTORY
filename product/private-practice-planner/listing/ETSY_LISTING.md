## Listing description

**PRIVATE PRACTICE PLANNER — EXCEL SPREADSHEET**

How many sliding scale clients can you actually afford to see?

Most practitioners answer that by feel. This workbook answers it with a number.

Enter what you charge, how many slots you hold each week, how often those slots are actually
kept, and what it costs to keep your practice open. The workbook works out what your caseload
earns, what you take home after the tax you set aside, and how many of your weekly slots can be
at a reduced fee while you still reach your income target.

**WHAT IT WORKS OUT**

• What your current caseload earns across a year
• What one calendar slot is really worth once no-shows are counted
• Your total practice costs, monthly and annual
• What you take home after costs and your own tax set-aside
• How far that is above or below the income target you set
• The revenue you need to reach that target
• Sessions needed to cover your costs, and to reach your target
• Slots per week needed to reach your target
• **How many reduced-fee slots you can carry and still get there**

**A SLOT AND A SESSION ARE NOT THE SAME THING**

A slot is a place on your calendar. A session is one that happened.

At 90% attendance, a $150 slot is worth $135. Every figure that matters follows from that
difference, and it is the reason caseload planning done on fee alone comes out wrong.

The workbook tracks both. It shows your average fee per session held and your average per slot
on the calendar, and it uses the per-slot figure wherever the question is about how many hours
you need to work.

**SLIDING SCALE, AS A NUMBER**

Set your full fee, the reduced fee you want to offer, your weekly slots and your attendance
rate. The workbook calculates how many of those slots can be at the reduced fee with your target
still reached.

It will also tell you when the answer is none — that at the target you have set, every slot
needs to be at your full fee. That is often the most useful thing it says.

**BUILT FOR MIXED FEE STRUCTURES**

Six fee tiers, so full fee, two sliding scale rates and insurance can sit side by side, each
with its own attendance rate. Insurance slots and private pay slots do not behave the same way,
and the workbook does not pretend they do.

**WHAT YOU RECEIVE**

• Private Practice Planner — Microsoft Excel workbook (.xlsx), 4 tabs
• A one-page Start Here guide
• A reference tab explaining every term and every editable assumption
• Instant digital download

**MADE FOR**

Therapists, counselors, social workers, psychologists, coaches and other solo practitioners who
set their own fees and want the numbers behind their caseload rather than a feeling about them.

**PLEASE NOTE**

This is a Microsoft Excel workbook (.xlsx) designed for desktop Excel. It downloads straight to
your computer — there is no account to create and nothing to share. Google Sheets compatibility
is not included or supported.

This workbook does arithmetic on the numbers you enter. It does not recommend a fee, a tax rate,
a caseload or a sliding scale policy, and it contains nothing clinical. The tax set-aside is a
rate you choose — check it with your accountant.

Every workbook is developed and rigorously quality-checked with assistance from frontier AI
models, helping catch errors, test calculations and deliver a more reliable tool for your
business.

This is a digital product. No physical item will be shipped.
