Nursing study sheet

DIABETES MELLITUS

The body cannot make insulin, cannot use it well, or both — so glucose stays in the blood instead of entering the cells.

Type 1

NO insulin  ·  autoimmune destruction of beta cells
  • Absolute insulin deficiency — insulin is required for life
  • Often diagnosed young, but can occur at any age
  • Rapid onset; often thin or weight loss at diagnosis
  • Crisis risk: DKA

Type 2

RESISTANCE to insulin  ·  plus relative deficiency
  • Cells respond poorly to insulin; production falls over time
  • Most common type; risk rises with age, excess weight, inactivity, family history
  • Gradual onset; may have no symptoms for years
  • Lifestyle, oral or injectable meds; may need insulin. Crisis risk: HHS

Hypoglycemia · below 70 mg/dL

"Cold and clammy — need some candy"
  • Shaky, sweaty, pale, cool clammy skin
  • Tachycardia, anxiety, hunger, irritability
  • Confusion, slurred speech → seizure, loss of consciousness
  • Rule of 15: 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate → recheck in 15 min → repeat if still under 70. Then a snack or meal
  • Unable to swallow safely: glucagon or IV dextrose per protocol — nothing by mouth

Hyperglycemia

"Hot and dry — sugar high"
  • 3 Ps: polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia
  • Warm, dry skin; dehydration
  • Blurred vision, fatigue, headache
  • Slow wound healing, frequent infections
  • Untreated → DKA (type 1) or HHS (type 2)

Diagnosis (ADA)

A1C≥ 6.5%
Fasting glucose≥ 126 mg/dL
2-h OGTT≥ 200 mg/dL
Random + symptoms≥ 200 mg/dL
Prediabetes A1C5.7–6.4%
A1C reflects average glucose over about 3 months.

Nursing priorities

  • Check glucose before meals and at bedtime, or as ordered
  • Coordinate insulin with meal trays
  • Rotate injection sites within one area
  • Watch for hypoglycemia when NPO, vomiting, or at insulin peak

Sick-day rules

  • Do not stop insulin when ill, even if not eating
  • Check glucose more often
  • Check ketones, especially type 1
  • Fluids to prevent dehydration; report persistent vomiting

Foot care teaching

  • Inspect feet daily; use a mirror
  • Never go barefoot; well-fitting shoes
  • Wash and dry well, especially between toes
  • Report cuts, blisters, color change
