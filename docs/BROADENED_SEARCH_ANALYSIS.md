## The diagnosis, and it is structural

**Q14 — are our rules forcing Factory toward marketplaces? Yes.**

The Stranger Arrival Test demands "a named surface with measurable evidence people use it."
Marketplaces satisfy that trivially and almost nothing else does. So the *gate itself* selects
for marketplaces.

The rule is right; my reading was wrong. I treated "named surface" as **"an existing
marketplace"** rather than **"a mechanism Factory can operate"** — which includes surfaces
Factory *creates*, such as programmatically generated indexed pages.

**Q15 — are our rules optimizing for one product when the advantage is a portfolio? Yes.**
Campaign discipline exists to accumulate a denominator across repeated attempts. I read it as
"find the one right niche."

**Q1 — why conventional products and marketplaces?** Because I searched for markets **as if
Factory were a generic entrant**. I never asked what Factory uniquely has. That was the core
error, and it is upstream of every other one.

## The central asymmetry

> **AI collapses CREATION cost to ~zero. It does not collapse DISTRIBUTION cost.**

This single fact explains the entire history of this project. Marketplaces kept winning because
**they bundle discovery** — they are the only surface where per-asset distribution cost is also
near zero.

It follows that a portfolio model works only where distribution is *also* near-zero marginal
cost per asset. Exactly three shapes qualify:

| Shape | Per-asset distribution cost | Time to signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace built-in discovery** | ~$0.20 listing | **21–30 days** |
| **Programmatic search surface** — one system, many indexed pages | ~$0 after shared setup | 90–120 days |
| **Platform galleries / directories** | ~$0 per submission | 30–60 days |

Everything else — social, outreach, content marketing — requires per-asset human effort and is
therefore structurally incompatible with Factory's mission, not merely disallowed by the gate.

## Answers

**Q2 — what becomes interesting because Factory automates the whole chain?** Business models
whose *unit economics are destroyed by human labour cost*: products worth $5–30 that take a
human hours to make; niches too small for a human to serve profitably; products needing per-
customer variation; assets whose expected value is $10/month, which no human would build but a
portfolio of 100 makes material.

**Q3 — why isn't autonomous landing-page generation core? It should be, and this is the
largest single miss.** A Factory-built page is fully instrumented (no platform reporting
limits), un-gated by any reviewer, owned rather than rented, and **lets the offer be tested
before the product exists**. What stopped me was distribution — a bare page has no discovery.
The resolution is *programmatic* surfaces: one generator producing many genuinely useful
indexed pages. Represented here by families `programmatic-site`, `micro-tool`,
`affiliate-asset`, `content-to-commerce`.

**Q4 — digitally creatable and fulfillable formats:** workbooks, worksheets, templates,
reports, question banks, datasets, feeds, calculators, generators, personalised documents,
reference pages, directories, audits, SDKs, blueprint packs, media asset packs. All 16 families
in the portfolio.

**Q5 — productized services viable when structured input → automatic output:** accessibility
audits, listing SEO audits, feed health checks, menu margin analysis, readability scoring,
personalised training plans, custom patterns graded to measurements, wiring diagrams from load
inputs. The pattern: **the customer supplies the variation, so Factory never improvises.**

**Q6 — monetization without selling a conventional product:** affiliate commission on
comparison and fitment assets, lead fees, ad-supported free utilities, paid data access,
sponsored placement on genuinely useful references, licensing a generator to agencies. 9
distinct monetization mechanisms are represented.

**Q7 — product IS a web page/app:** the `micro-tool`, `programmatic-site` and
`content-to-commerce` families. Advantage: complete funnel instrumentation and no gatekeeper.
Cost: distribution must be earned, which takes 90+ days.

**Q10 — models benefiting most from marginal cost → 0:** per-customer personalisation, many
small variations over one template, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction reference material, and
audits where analysis was previously the expensive part.

**Q11 — least ongoing owner labour after setup:** instant-file marketplace downloads (zero
touch), then generated-on-demand outputs, then static programmatic pages. Worst: anything with
an uptime obligation or a support surface.

**Q12 — testable before building:** `content-to-commerce` and every `hosted-page` model —
publish the offer page and measure intent first. **Ethical constraint: no fake checkout.** A
pre-order must carry a real delivery commitment, and a waitlist must be labelled as one.
Deceptive buy buttons are prohibited outright.

**Q13 — distribution Factory can operate without the owner becoming a marketer:** marketplace
search, programmatic search-indexed pages, directory and gallery submission, API marketplace
listings, package registries, feed integrations. **Not** posting, networking, cold-messaging,
or content creation.

## Q8/Q9 — portfolio arithmetic, done explicitly

```
portfolio profit = (profit per asset × assets) − portfolio operating cost
```

**Marketplace digital download, per asset:** price $12 → **net $10.41** after fees.
Creation cost ≈ $0.20 listing + model inference. Maintenance ≈ 0 (instant delivery, no uptime).

| Assets | Hit rate (≥1 sale/mo) | Monthly gross | Listing renewals | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 10% | $52 | ~$2.50 | **~$50** |
| 50 | 20% | $104 | ~$2.50 | **~$100** |
| 100 | 20% | $208 | ~$5 | **~$200** |
| 100 | 20% @ 2 sales/mo | $416 | ~$5 | **~$410** |

**What must be true for 100 assets to work:**

1. **Creation cost stays under ~$1 including inference.** Plausible but unmeasured.
2. **Hit rate ≥10%.** Completely unknown — this is the number the first test exists to find.
3. **Maintenance stays ~0 per asset.** True for instant files; false for anything hosted.
4. **The platform tolerates the volume.** A new shop publishing 100 listings may trip
   spam/quality enforcement. **This is the binding constraint and it is unverified.**
5. **Failure is detectable per asset** — Etsy reports impressions per listing, so yes.

**The honest conclusion:** portfolio economics are *plausible but entirely unproven*, and hinge
on a hit rate nobody can research. It is measurable for **$0.20 per attempt**, which is why
running assets beats studying them.

## Factory as the reusable asset

The owner's closing reframe is, I think, correct and I had it backwards.

I have been treating each candidate as *the* business, and Factory as scaffolding for finding
it. The evidence points the other way: **no single asset examined has a high ceiling**, but
creating and testing another one is extremely cheap — and getting cheaper as the machinery
accumulates.

That inverts the success criterion. The question is not "did Factory find a good business?" but
**"is Factory's cost-per-tested-business low enough that a low hit rate still compounds?"**

Concretely, the durable assets are the generator, the funnel instrumentation, the evidence
semantics gate, the kill discipline, and the listing/deployment pipeline — none of which is
specific to any niche, and all of which make attempt N+1 cheaper than attempt N.

**This does not lower the evidence bar.** Each asset still needs real strangers, real
denominators, and real money. It changes what "success" means: **a working, cheap, repeatable
loop with a measured hit rate**, rather than one product that worked.

**What is still unproven, and cannot be researched:** the hit rate, the true creation cost
including inference, and platform tolerance for volume. All three are answered by running
assets — starting at $0.20.
